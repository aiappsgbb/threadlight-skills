From intelligence to accountable action

Autonomy for agents.
Not unlimited power.

Let agents move work forward while your organization defines the boundaries. Bring identity, business policy, controlled execution, human judgement and evidence into one configurable architecture.

The model proposes; the system authorizes. Build useful autonomy around the decisions that matter to your business, with clear responsibility from the first request to the resulting action.

A connected operating model

Give every business action a clear decision path.

Choose the controls your workflow needs and connect them at the action boundary. Agents contribute intelligence; your systems retain authority.

  1. Business intent

    Turn a request into a proposed action with explicit inputs and an intended outcome. Keep the purpose of the workflow visible.

  2. Identity

    Establish which agent, person and service are participating. Give each the access needed for its responsibility.

  3. Policy

    Express business rules against trusted context and current system data. Evaluate the proposed action before it can change anything.

  4. Human decisions

    Route exceptions to the people accountable for them. Record an authenticated decision and bind it to the action being reviewed.

  5. Controlled execution

    Use a trusted host or gateway and a separately authorizing backend to turn an allowed proposal into a business outcome.

  6. Evidence

    Connect the request, policy decision, human involvement and system result. Give operations and reviewers a trace they can inspect.

One journey, distinct responsibilities. Model access, action authorization and business execution work together without becoming the same permission. Integrate controls where they matter, and keep routine reads and prototype iteration lightweight.

Threadlight connects native agent runtimes, governed MCP boundaries and existing business APIs. Implementation profiles make the supported controls and deployment choices explicit.

Separate responsibilities, not interchangeable security labels
  • SAFE is the method; ACS/Rego is the policy decision point.
  • Agent Hooks is the host/interceptor contract. The selected host or gateway is the enforcement point; AGT is the toolkit; ASSERT is assurance.
  • Agent Identity, reviewer, control plane, gateway, downstream caller, database writer and publisher have distinct responsibilities. The agent has no database-write or signing role.
  • Private networks restrict reachability; output guardrails constrain supported returned content. Neither replaces authorization of the exact business effect.

PRODUCTIVE AUTONOMY

Delegate the repeatable work

Let agents gather context, propose a disposition and carry out permitted actions. Put business rules at the boundary where a proposal becomes an effect.

  • Define actions around clear business outcomes.
  • Use current facts from the systems that own them.
  • Keep access and execution responsibilities separate.
DECISIONS AT THE RIGHT LEVEL

Bring people into the exceptions

Set thresholds, roles and approval conditions that reflect how your organization works. Keep routine decisions moving and reserve human attention for consequential exceptions.

Native Outlook approval brings the decision to the reviewer. The control plane verifies the person's response; the agent resumes only the operation that was reviewed.

ACCOUNTABLE OPERATIONS

Understand how an outcome happened

Connect agent intent, policy decisions, approvals and system results. Make the evidence useful to engineering, operations and accountable business owners.

Design the right audit guarantees for each boundary, from data access to a committed transaction and subsequent reconciliation.

EXAMPLE WORKFLOW

Returns triage

An agent reads an authorized case, recommends a disposition and checks the proposal against eligibility, value and risk rules. Exceptions go to a supervisor.

The business system records the allowed decision and its audit. Extend the workflow through explicit backend contracts rather than handing the agent a general-purpose write tool.

YOUR SYSTEMS OF RECORD

Connect without surrendering control

Keep business APIs responsible for their own authorization and data integrity. Use agent tools as scoped contracts, with clear inputs, outcomes and ownership.

This pattern lets the agent experience evolve while business systems retain the rules that protect their records.

AN EVOLVING OPERATING MODEL

Govern the change, not just the first release

Review policies, identities and execution boundaries as your use case grows. Pair agent quality and safety evaluation with operational evidence and clear release ownership.

Human judgement, without a disconnected handoff

Decide in Outlook.
Execute under policy.

Put consequential exceptions in front of an accountable person. Keep their decision connected to the exact business action, all the way to its recorded outcome.

  1. Review a clear proposal

    See the case, requested action, proposed reason and deadline in an approval email. The recipient and review rules come from trusted configuration, not the model.

  2. Choose Approve or Reject

    Use the native Outlook experience. The control plane independently checks the returned identity and binds the decision to the original proposal.

  3. Resume only that operation

    An approved request receives one-use authorization. The gateway and business API still enforce current conditions, durable audit and duplicate-effect protection.

A decision, not a blank cheque. Changed data or arguments need a new authorization path. A request past its deadline does not silently renew, and a completed retry must not become another business effect. The email workflow itself has no business-write action.

FOR THE CISO

Know who authorized which effect

Separate identity, policy, human authority and execution. Trace the reviewed proposal to its authorization, business outcome and replay behavior.

Build for change.
Keep authority aligned.

Identity and policy travel with the deployment

Bind reviewed configuration to the agent and environment that will use it. Version the rules, know who can publish them and re-establish authority when the system changes.

Evidence supports the next decision

Give business and platform teams a shared view of execution, exceptions and operational requirements. Use that evidence to decide where to expand autonomy and where to keep review.

Prototype quickly. Adopt controls deliberately. Start with the business process, select the actions that matter and apply the appropriate implementation profile before connecting consequential effects.

Your business process.
A clear path to adoption.

  • Define the outcome. Name the process, the decisions and the people accountable for them.
  • Select the boundaries. Identify the tools, identities, policies and backend contracts the workflow needs.
  • Connect the experience. Bring agent assistance, human decisions and business execution into a coherent journey.
  • Prepare the operating model. Align evaluation, privacy, recovery and release ownership with your production requirements.

One return. Three decision paths.

Follow a permitted action, a blocked proposal or a human-reviewed exception.

  2. Policy decides
  3. Blocked — no business effect
  4. Outlook review — this handoff only
  5. Fresh checks — same proposal
  6. Audit ACK — before the effect
  7. Backend checks + writes
