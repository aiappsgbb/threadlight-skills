Production-ready

From agent potential to operational confidence.

Bring business owners, engineering and operations around a clear path to adoption. threadlight-production-ready connects the architecture, operating requirements and evidence your organization needs to make the next decision.

It scores the agent across 13 production pillars, names the skill or owner for each gap, and gives remediation + delivery the evidence they need to move the pilot toward ship — not skip the hardening work.

Start with the use case and its risks. Platform security, model governance, agent behavior, quality, data protection and operations need distinct controls and accountable owners. None replaces the others.

Why it matters

A demo isn’t a production system.

A green demo doesn’t prove the CISO, SRE and FinOps can sign off. Without that evidence the customer defers — and the pilot dies in the lab.

The stall

“It demos” isn’t “it’s governed.”

No identity, audit trail, eval gate or cost ceiling — nothing a review board can sign. So the pilot waits.

Complementary responsibilities

Production readiness is not a single control.

A private network does not establish answer quality. A safe model response does not authorize a business transaction. A correctly authorized transaction does not establish service reliability. Platform and network controls

Where can the system run, connect and authenticate?

Landing zones, private ingress and egress, workload identity, IAM and secret management constrain exposure and service access. The platform and security teams own these boundaries; Threadlight must integrate with them, not replace them.

Review: permitted routes and identities, least privilege and the absence of unintended direct access. A permitted connection still does not approve every action sent over it.

Model governance

Which models may be used, under which constraints?

Approved models and endpoints, content safety, model-facing gateway policies, quotas and lifecycle decisions control consumption and supported safety risks. The AI platform and responsible-AI owners define the policy; model changes also require quality review.

Review: approved access, configured safety controls, usage limits and model-change criteria. A model gateway does not govern downstream business tools unless those actions are explicitly routed and controlled.

Agent behavior governance

May this agent perform this particular business action now?

An agent can use an approved model and identity yet propose an ineligible decision or act on stale information. The business owner specifies action rules and required human review; engineering places enforcement before the selected tool can change data.

Review: exact action scope, trusted facts, human authority when required and the resulting transaction. This framework covers selected action paths, not every internal model behavior.

Quality and evaluation

Does the agent solve the intended problem reliably?

Business acceptance cases, retrieval and citation checks, regression evaluations and adversarial tests assess usefulness and failure behavior. Product and engineering owners set thresholds and representative test data rather than accepting a convincing demo.

Review: task success, safety failures and changes against a baseline. Evaluation informs a release decision; it is not a substitute for authorization during each selected action.

Information protection

Whose data may be retrieved, disclosed and retained?

Source permissions, retrieval access controls, data minimization and retention rules apply to prompts, responses, memory and audit. Data and privacy owners determine the allowed scope. A shared service identity must not erase the caller’s access boundaries.

Review: denied-access cases, disclosure paths and sensitive logging. Recording an authorization decision does not make its business payload safe to expose or retain indefinitely.

Grounding and retrieval access checks →

Operations and lifecycle

Can the organization run, afford, change and recover this service?

SLOs, telemetry, capacity tests, cost per successful interaction, incident ownership and rollback plans make the workload operable. SRE, FinOps and delivery owners also manage dependency provenance and evidence gates for changes.

Review: tested recovery, budget limits, accountable on-call ownership and release criteria. Rolling back agent code does not undo a completed business transaction; that needs its own recovery procedure.

Inside one domain: agent behavior governance

Access to a system is not authority for every action.

An authenticated agent on a private network can still propose an ineligible return, use an outdated case or repeat a decision after a timeout. The missing question is not “can it call this API?” but “may it perform this exact action, with these facts, now?”

Builds on your existing controls Identity & accessContent safety & model gatewaysNetwork isolation

Example: a returns assistant, not a payment authority.

Ordinary eligible case: record the recommendation automatically when policy permits. High-value case: pause for an authorized reviewer to approve or reject a supervisor handoff. Changed case: stop rather than apply a decision to an obsolete revision. Repeated completed request: retrieve the recorded outcome, not another write.

The existing business API keeps its eligibility and transaction rules. Threadlight adds a consistent agent-facing contract: bind the exact proposal to policy, trusted facts, any required human decision and an authorization record before dispatch. The reference records a case decision and its audit; it does not move money.

Routine work

Keep permitted cases automatic within explicit business rules instead of requiring manual approval for every tool call.

Exceptions

Let the right person decide a specific proposal in Outlook, without granting the agent general authority to override the rules.

Accountability

Connect the policy and human decision to the actual business outcome, so an operator can explain and reconcile what happened.

  1. Proposals stay proposals

    The agent cannot grant itself permission, choose a writer credential or change the approved target.

  2. Rules meet real facts

    Signed policy and trusted business evidence constrain the exact action, not just its explanation.

  3. People decide in Outlook

    When review is required, the human approves or rejects that proposal. Pending is not permission.

  4. Authority precedes action

    Any required one-use approval and the durable authorization record must be acknowledged before the selected write.

  5. The business system checks again

    A separate identity and conditional transaction protect the current record. Unknown outcomes are reconciled, not blindly retried.

  6. Replay is not another effect

    A completed operation returns its verified outcome. It does not create a second business change.

A selected boundary, not a blanket claim. Explicit opt-in covers configured actions; invalid selected configuration fails closed. An unbound read remains usable without ACS, under its normal access rules, with optional audit before return. Other tools, quality, resilience and network isolation need their own controls.

What it checks

13 pillars, three architectures, one detected posture.

A scorecard is evidence for a decision, not the decision itself. Review the raw and waiver-adjusted scores, verification coverage and dates together. Missing permissions or unexecuted checks remain not-verified; a waiver is accepted risk, not technical enforcement.

raw_score, score_with_waivers, would_fail_hard_gate, verification coverage and captured_at belong beside stale evidence and outstanding must-fix findings. The scorecard is advisory; protected evidence gates and human go-live approval remain separate.

Different owners, complementary controls

The platform team owns the landing zone — including its network, identities and any shared AI gateway. Threadlight organizes the workload’s readiness review across these 13 pillars, with scoped evidence and named owners. Runtime action controls, evaluations, a supply-chain SBOM, an agent-identity AI-BOM and regulatory evidence serve different purposes. None alone proves the whole agent is production-ready or establishes legal compliance.

Confidence through a connected operating model

From agent potential to accountable outcomes.

Bring business owners, engineering and operations around the same workflow. Identity, policy, human decisions and audit create a practical path from a promising prototype to an application your organization can operate.

Design for the business

Make autonomy useful

Start with the process, its decisions and the systems of record. Give agents clear tasks and connect the actions that create value through explicit contracts.

Let routine work move forward inside your rules while keeping responsibility for consequential outcomes with the right systems and people.

Decide at the right level

Human judgement, connected

Define which exceptions require review and who can decide them. Notification channels can guide reviewers to the right context; authenticated approval records the decision that permits the workflow to continue.

The decision belongs to the reviewer. The execution belongs to a controlled system path, with a clear relationship between the two.

Operate with evidence

Connect actions to outcomes

Trace the request, the policy decision, human involvement and the result recorded by the business system. Give operations and reviewers evidence they can use.

Bring this action-level view together with quality, reliability, privacy and cost evidence to guide the next release and the next expansion of autonomy.

Target architecture

What production looks like.

A proposed customer target, not the S3 lab topology: model traffic can use an APIM hub and the Foundry project a private, keyless spoke. Model access and selected effect authority are separate boundaries; correlate their evidence rather than assuming one audit chain from topology alone.

Recap

Fast prototypes. Reviewable production evidence.

Explicit effect authority is one part of readiness. Color is a summary, not the contract: pass, should-fix, must-fix, not-applicable, not-verified and waived remain distinct findings.

Green

Evidence found — the pillar is verified, and the proof is committed next to your code.

Amber

A gap needs an owner and remediation. Any accepted waiver needs explicit scope, conditions and a review date; it is not evidence of technical closure.

Not-verified

Could not verify it — for example, missing access or unexecuted checks. Advisory reporting and explicit strict readiness gates have different exit behavior; unverified evidence is not a pass.

A selected path can be enforced while another remains unbound or unverified. The advisory scorecard does not hide must-fix findings; production requires current evidence and accountable owners beyond the demonstration.
