01 / Platform and shared services

Many use cases. One accountable AI platform.

Citadel brings model access, network integration, telemetry and consumption governance into a shared hub. Teams keep ownership of their agents without rebuilding that foundation for every project.

Example / shared AI capacity
Three teams use the same model pool. An analytics batch consumes the capacity that customer support needs.

Who owns the consumption, which use case should receive capacity, and which model policies apply? Separate API credentials and three spreadsheets are not a shared operating model.

Explore Citadel Governance Hub →Read the Citadel blueprint →

Already owned by your platform: landing zones, network isolation, identity, IAM and secrets. Threadlight conforms to that foundation; it does not replace it.

Use the hub. Keep the boundaries.

Threadlight supports the workload’s integration and deployment posture; the platform team owns the hub. Model access, source-data access and business writes remain separate paths.

Where access control breaks: exposure, privilege and bypasses
Unintended exposure
A public service or permissive ingress creates another entry point. Control: inventory reachable endpoints, constrain ingress and verify DNS/private routes. A private endpoint alone does not close every public path.
Overpowered identity
A developer credential works but can modify unrelated records. Control: use service identities with distinct reader, dispatcher and writer permissions; remove embedded secrets and test denied operations.
Alternate tool path
A custom client or direct database route avoids the selected gateway. Control: inventory every consequential path and close bypasses or report them explicitly as uncovered. One governed tool is not whole-agent protection.
Wrong destination
A proposed URL sends data outside the intended service. Control: trusted code selects backend targets; constrain outbound access and recheck the deployed route rather than trusting template intent.

Accountable teams: platform engineering and security. Review: permitted routes, least privilege and unintended direct access.

Platform / model services

Make the model route and its consumer explicit

In the Citadel posture, the shared APIM hub governs the configured model route: access, usage limits and supported safety policies. These are platform services, not the new business-action authorization layer.

Already owned by the AI platform: approved models, model-facing gateway policies, content safety, quotas and lifecycle decisions. These do not automatically govern downstream business tools.

A shared gateway, not a shared blank cheque.

Make permitted models, safety and usage limits explicit for each consumer. Evaluation of a replacement model belongs to the release process; this contract governs how the model service is consumed.

Where model governance breaks: drift, bypass and false confidence
Unapproved selection
A fallback routes to a model or region outside the agreed policy. Control: constrain permitted destinations and make fallback behavior explicit; inspect the effective runtime configuration.
Safeguards bypassed
A client calls an alternate endpoint without required filters or usage limits. Control: enforce policies on the actual model route and test both accepted and rejected requests.
Silent lifecycle change
A retirement, version change or incompatible runtime alters behavior. Control: track compatibility and lifecycle dates, evaluate a replacement and plan migration before the dependency disappears.
Safe-looking, wrong answer
The model produces acceptable language but invents facts. Control: evaluate task correctness and grounding separately. Content filtering is not business-rule enforcement.

Accountable teams: AI platform and responsible AI. Review: approved access, safety configuration and change criteria.

Platform and application / data boundaries

Data access and privacy: existing responsibilities

These controls are not supplied by the new action-governance runtime. Source services and application owners enforce user/tenant access, memory isolation and retention. Citadel or a private network does not grant permission to read every record.

Already owned by data and privacy teams: source permissions, permitted uses, minimization and retention rules. Encryption or a private endpoint does not decide which records this user may see.

Filter before context. Minimize after.

Keep caller authorization attached to retrieval. Threadlight assesses supplied access, citation and refusal evidence; the application and data services enforce access. Its grounding workflow is not a retrieval engine.

Where data escapes: retrieval, memory, instructions and logs
Service-wide retrieval
A broad index or service account bypasses user permissions. Control: enforce caller/tenant scope before records enter context, and test cross-user and denied-access cases.
Shared memory
A later conversation receives facts outside its permitted scope. Control: isolate sessions, caches and persistent memory by the intended access boundary; test reuse and expiry.
Evidence becomes instruction
A retrieved document says to disclose details or ignore controls. Control: treat retrieved text as untrusted evidence, test adversarial disclosure, and keep source authorization and action authority outside the model.
Logs become a second leak
Prompts, traces or audit payloads retain personal data beyond their purpose. Control: minimize fields, restrict operator access, define deletion/retention and review locality and legal obligations. A scorecard does not establish compliance.

Accountable teams: data owners and privacy. Review: denied-access cases, disclosure paths and sensitive logging.

Fit the existing estate

Use Citadel where it fits. Respect the platform already in place.

A shared hub is valuable across teams. A standalone workload or an estate with an existing gateway may use a different posture. These are platform choices, not different guarantees of whole-agent governance.

02 / AgentOps and release

Different estates. A deliberate path to release.

Moving from development to production means fitting the customer’s deployment model, not copying a developer’s permissions. Threadlight provides deployment artifacts and CI/CD blueprints, then connects the candidate to evaluation, red-team and explicit gate decisions.

Example / the pilot works, but the delivery model changes
The agent runs in the pilot subscription. Production permits deployment only through the customer’s approved pipeline.

Keep the workload design; change who deploys, the target configuration and the verification contract. A model, prompt or tool change also needs fresh quality and safety evidence — even if the container still starts.

DevSecOps owns release assurance: check configuration and policy offline, execute the required evaluations, and verify the deployed candidate before the go-live decision. Business-action authorization remains the active runtime control, not a pipeline verdict.

02 / The generated deployment path

Deploy through CI/CD — not your laptop.

Generate the pipeline and runbooks for the platform team: federated identity, scoped RBAC and no secret in the repo. The deploy job changes the target environment. The verdict jobs below run after deployment; they do not sit in front of it.

Generated execution versus integration work. Provision/deploy commands and verdict readers are executable. In the base templates, the evaluation, red-team and MCP evidence-producer steps are still echo instructions. Connect actual producers and fresh artifact delivery before treating those readers as evidence of this deployment.

For explicitly opted-in agents, the optional AgentOps composition replaces the evaluation placeholder with a real bound batch and assessor. It does not automatically wire the red-team or MCP producers.

Soft — the default

A failing verdict step is allowed to continue. A successful workflow does not imply all controls passed.

Hard — explicit selection

The verdict step fails on a missing file or a rejected verdict. Evaluation accepts comprehensive or partial; red-team accepts hardened or partial. MCP checks summary.must_fix, not an automatic live re-scan. These base readers do not validate freshness or deployment binding.

What the platform team configures

Deployment authority is configured separately from evidence policy.
--platform
github-actions · azure-devops

GitHub Actions or Azure DevOps — the pipeline is emitted for your CI.

--eval-gatesoft · hard

Sets both evaluation and red-team verdict handling after deploy. It is not a merge-protection setting.

--mcp-gatesoft · hard

Sets handling of the MCP SBOM summary. Producing a current scan is a separate integration step.

--central-env-required
standalone · existing hub · separate hub deployment

Resolve the onboarding path and target-resource scope. This workload pipeline never deploys the shared Citadel hub.

--private-network
self-hosted · managed pool

Select private-network runners. DNS, routes and permitted egress still need platform-team configuration and verification.

01 / Candidate checks

A valid configuration is not a successful evaluation.

A prompt or model change can pass unit tests and still choose the wrong tool or invent evidence. Compare the candidate with the baseline on the same cases, with expected outcomes and explicit thresholds.

Inspect offline

Check compiled infrastructure, policy/schema validity, tool inventory and declared evaluation assets. Executed local conformance adds local behavior evidence — not cloud or business-write proof.

Run the evaluation

An evaluator must invoke and score the candidate. Keep datasets, thresholds and actual run results. The manifest assessor reads that evidence; finding an evaluation script is not executing it.

Separate checks: quality evaluation, adversarial testing and MCP supply-chain inspection answer different questions. Content filtering does not replace any of them.

Make the result consumable, not just visible.

threadlight-evals assesses scenarios, datasets, run history, thresholds and freshness into specs/evals-manifest.json. Its --gate exits 2 on a must-fix capability; continuous wiring, alerts and A/B evidence are assessed separately. The optional opted-in AgentOps path can execute a bound batch; it does not supply every missing capability.

Where quality breaks: weak evidence, blind spots and regressions

Right answer, bad evidence
The recommendation happens to be right but its citation is irrelevant or fabricated. Control: assess retrieval relevance, citation support and refusal when evidence is insufficient.
Demo-only coverage
Happy-path examples miss uncommon tasks, languages, accessibility needs or ambiguous requests. Control: build representative cases and judge failure severity with business owners.
Hostile input
Instructions inside a document try to redirect tools or disclose data. Control: run adversarial tests and check actual tool/data behavior, not just the model’s reassuring explanation.
Average hides regression
A faster candidate improves easy tasks but fails critical edge cases. Control: compare slices and must-not-fail cases against the baseline; monitor drift after release and retain a rollback decision.

Accountable teams: product and engineering. Review: task success, failure severity and regression against the agreed baseline.

Before go-live / on the deployed candidate

A deployment succeeded. Does the intended workload work?

An evaluation against a mock can pass while the real API denies the user or the deployed image uses the wrong identity. Verify the intended environment and the actual paths before accepting the release.

Coverage is specific. A noop probe is not business-write proof. Verifying one selected binding does not verify every tool. Local conformance, live observations and business execution remain distinct evidence.

The control continues after release. Once installed, the action-governance runtime authorizes selected effects on each invocation. A pre-go-live test or a CI soft setting does not replace that enforcement.

Separate, authorized probes: connect verifies a real endpoint; ground assesses caller-provided ACL/citation/refusal evidence; loadtest requires an explicit budget and live-run authorization. These are not automatically executed by the base deploy template.

03 / Active governance in production

Access to a system is not authority for every action.

An authenticated agent on a private network can still propose an ineligible return, use an outdated case or repeat a decision after a timeout. The missing question is not “can it call this API?” but “may it perform this exact action, with these facts, now?”

Example / a customer return
“Can I return this order?”
The agent’s job

Read the case and current policy, recommend an outcome or request a supervisor decision, then record the result with an audit.

A decision record, not a payment. The example does not settle a refund.

Already protected: network isolation, identity and model access. The existing business API keeps its eligibility and transaction rules. The remaining gap is authority for the exact proposed action.

Put authority before the write.

Bind selected tool calls to business policy, trusted facts, human review when required and a durable authorization record before execution. The agent proposes; it cannot grant itself permission.

Routine workEligible case → permitted recommendation

ExceptionsHigh-value case → reviewed supervisor handoff

AccountabilityRepeated request → existing outcome, not another write

  1. Proposals stay proposals

    The agent cannot grant itself permission, choose a writer credential or change the approved target.

  2. Rules meet real facts

    Signed policy and trusted business evidence constrain the exact action, not just its explanation.

  3. People decide in Outlook

    When review is required, the human approves or rejects that proposal. Pending is not permission.

  4. Authority precedes action

    Any required one-use approval and the durable authorization record must be acknowledged before the selected write.

  5. The business system checks again

    A separate identity and conditional transaction protect the current record. Unknown outcomes are reconciled, not blindly retried.

  6. Replay is not another effect

    A completed operation returns its verified outcome. It does not create a second business change.

A selected boundary, not a blanket claim. Explicit opt-in covers configured actions; invalid selected configuration fails closed. An unbound read remains usable without ACS, under its normal access rules, with optional audit before return. The returns reference records a recommendation or handoff, not a payment or financial settlement. Other tools, quality, resilience and network isolation need their own controls.

Where action authority breaks: invented approval, stale facts and replay
Invented permission
A retrieved note says “ignore approval” or the model submits approved: true. Control: strict schemas and trusted policy/facts, not model-supplied authority. The tool cannot choose its writer credential or target.
Facts changed
The case was eligible when read but changed before dispatch. Control: recheck authorization after waits; the business API validates current state and commits conditionally. A changed proposal needs new authority.
Wrong or old approval
A forwarded email, stale click or approval for another case is treated as permission. Control: verify the responder, role, exact proposal and freshness; consume required authority once. Pending or rejected proposals do not execute.
No durable authorization
The audit service is unavailable but the agent wants to continue. Control: required central audit ACK precedes the effect. A local log is not a substitute; keep the operation blocked.
Duplicate or uncertain write
A completed operation is submitted again, or its result is lost. Control: reuse the verified outcome for an authorized replay; retain reservations and reconcile uncertainty. This is not distributed exactly-once delivery.

From agent potential to accountable outcomes.

Bring business owners, engineering and operations around the same workflow. Identity, policy, human decisions and audit create a practical path from a promising prototype to an application your organization can operate.

Design for the business

Make autonomy useful

Start with the process, its decisions and the systems of record. Give agents clear tasks and connect the actions that create value through explicit contracts.

Let routine work move forward inside your rules while keeping responsibility for consequential outcomes with the right systems and people.

Decide at the right level

Human judgement, connected

Define which exceptions require review and who can decide them. Notification channels can guide reviewers to the right context; authenticated approval records the decision that permits the workflow to continue.

The decision belongs to the reviewer. The execution belongs to a controlled system path, with a clear relationship between the two.

Operate with evidence

Connect actions to outcomes

Trace the request, the policy decision, human involvement and the result recorded by the business system. Give operations and reviewers evidence they can use.

Bring this action-level view together with quality, reliability, privacy and cost evidence to guide the next release and the next expansion of autonomy.

Recap

Fast prototypes. Reviewable production evidence.

Explicit effect authority is one part of readiness. Color is a summary, not the contract: pass, should-fix, must-fix, not-applicable, not-verified and waived remain distinct findings.

Green

Evidence found — the pillar is verified, and the proof is committed next to your code.

Amber

A gap needs an owner and remediation. Any accepted waiver needs explicit scope, conditions and a review date; it is not evidence of technical closure.

Not-verified

Could not verify it — for example, missing access or unexecuted checks. Advisory reporting and explicit strict readiness gates have different exit behavior; unverified evidence is not a pass.

A selected path can be enforced while another remains unbound or unverified. The advisory scorecard does not hide must-fix findings; production requires current evidence and accountable owners beyond the demonstration.
